\section{Lagrangian Formulation}

The dynamics of the inverted pendulum are derived using the
Euler--Lagrange formulation. The Lagrangian is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = T - V,
    \label{eq:lagrangian}
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the total kinetic energy and $V$ is the gravitational
potential energy.

For a system with generalized coordinates $q_i$, the
Euler--Lagrange equations are
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}
    \left(
        \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}
        {\partial \dot{q}_i}
    \right)
    -
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}
    {\partial q_i}
    =
    Q_i,
    \label{eq:euler_lagrange}
\end{equation}
where $Q_i$ is the generalized nonconservative force associated with
$q_i$.

\subsection{Coordinate System}

The system is described using an inertial Cartesian coordinate frame
$\mathcal{F}_I$. The $x$ axis is horizontal and positive to the right,
while the $z$ axis is vertical and positive upward.

The position of the wheel axle is
\begin{equation}
    \bm{r}_{\mathrm{w}}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        x \\
        z
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eq:wheel_position}
\end{equation}
where $z$ is constant.

The center of mass of the pendulum body is located at
\begin{equation}
    \bm{r}_{\mathrm{b}}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        x_{\mathrm b} \\
        z_{\mathrm b}
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        x + \ell\sin\theta \\
        z + \ell\cos\theta
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eq:body_position}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the distance from the wheel axle to the center of mass
of the pendulum body.

The angle $\theta$ is measured clockwise from the positive $z$ axis.
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
    \theta = 0
\end{equation}
corresponds to the upright configuration of the inverted pendulum.

The generalized coordinates are
\begin{equation}
    q_1 = x,
    \qquad
    q_2 = \theta,
    \qquad
    q_3 = \psi,
    \qquad
    q_4 = \phi_{\mathrm w},
    \label{eq:generalized_coordinates}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{\mathrm w}$ is the angular position of the wheel.

The wheel angular velocity is
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{\mathrm w}
    =
    \dot{\phi}_{\mathrm w}.
    \label{eq:wheel_angular_velocity}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Rolling Constraint}

Assuming pure rolling without slipping, the wheel translational and
rotational velocities satisfy
\begin{equation}
    \dot{x}
    =
    r_{\mathrm w}\dot{\phi}_{\mathrm w},
    \label{eq:rolling_constraint_velocity}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\mathrm w}$ is the wheel radius.

The corresponding position constraint is
\begin{equation}
    x-r_{\mathrm w}\phi_{\mathrm w}=C,
    \label{eq:rolling_constraint_position}
\end{equation}
where $C$ is a constant determined by the initial configuration.

Because the rolling constraint eliminates one degree of freedom,
$\phi_{\mathrm w}$ may be eliminated from the final equations of motion
using
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\phi}_{\mathrm w}
    =
    \frac{\dot{x}}{r_{\mathrm w}},
    \qquad
    \ddot{\phi}_{\mathrm w}
    =
    \frac{\ddot{x}}{r_{\mathrm w}}.
    \label{eq:rolling_constraint_acceleration}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Kinetic Energy}

The translational kinetic energy of the wheel and pendulum body can
first be represented using the combined mass and the horizontal
velocity of the wheel axis. Since $z$ is constant,
\begin{equation*}
    \dot{z}=0.
\end{equation*}

The translational kinetic energy is therefore
\begin{equation}
    T_{\mathrm{trans}}
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    (m_{\mathrm b}+m_{\mathrm w})
    \dot{x}^{\,2},
    \label{eq:translational_kinetic_energy}
\end{equation}
where $m_{\mathrm b}$ and $m_{\mathrm w}$ are the masses of the
pendulum body and wheel assembly, respectively.

This expression does not account for the additional translational motion
of the pendulum body center of mass caused by its angular displacement.
The corresponding correction term is
\begin{equation}
    T_{\mathrm{corr}}
    =
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \dot{x}\dot{\theta}\cos\theta
    +
    \frac{1}{2}
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell^2
    \dot{\theta}^{\,2}.
    \label{eq:correction_kinetic_energy}
\end{equation}

The $\theta$ directional rotational kinetic energy of the pendulum body is
\begin{equation}
    T_{\mathrm b,rot}
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    I_{\mathrm b}
    \dot{\theta}^{\,2},
    \label{eq:body_rotational_energy}
\end{equation}
The $\psi$ directional rotational kinetic energy of the wheel is
\begin{align}
    T_{\mathrm{\psi,rot}}
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    \dot{\psi}^{\,2}
    \left(
        \sin^2\theta
        (I_{\mathrm{w}:1}+I_{\mathrm{b}:1})
        +\cos^2\theta(I_{\mathrm{w}:3}+I_{\mathrm{b}:3})
    \right)
\end{align}
and the rotational kinetic energy of the wheel is
\begin{equation}
    T_{\mathrm w,rot}
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    I_{\mathrm w}
    \dot{\phi}_{\mathrm w}^{\,2}.
    \label{eq:wheel_rotational_energy}
\end{equation}

Thus, the total kinetic energy is
\begin{align}
    T
    &=
    T_{\mathrm{trans}}
    +
    T_{\mathrm{corr}}
    +
    T_{\mathrm b,rot}
    +
    T_{\mathrm{\psi,rot}}
    +
    T_{\mathrm w,rot}
    \nonumber\\
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}
    (m_{\mathrm b}+m_{\mathrm w})
    \dot{x}^{\,2}
    +
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \dot{x}\dot{\theta}\cos\theta
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    \frac{1}{2}
    \left(
        I_{\mathrm{b}:2}
        +
        m_{\mathrm{b}}\ell^2
    \right)
    \dot{\theta}^{\,2}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    \frac{1}{2}
    I_{\mathrm{w}:2}
    \dot{\phi}_{\mathrm w}^{\,2}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    \frac{1}{2}
    \dot{\psi}^{\,2}
    \left(
        \sin^2\theta
        (I_{\mathrm{w}:1}+I_{\mathrm{b}:1} + m_{\mathrm b}\ell^2)
        +\cos^2\theta(I_{\mathrm{w}:3}+I_{\mathrm{b}:3})
    \right).
    \label{eq:total_kinetic_energy}
\end{align}

Applying the rolling constraint gives
\begin{align}
    T
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}
    \left(
        m_{\mathrm b}
        +
        m_{\mathrm w}
        +
        \frac{I_{\mathrm w}}{r_{\mathrm w}^{\,2}}
    \right)
    \dot{x}^{\,2}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \dot{x}\dot{\theta}\cos\theta
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    \frac{1}{2}
    \left(
        I_{\mathrm{b}:2}
        +
        m_{\mathrm b}\ell^2
    \right)
    \dot{\theta}^{\,2}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    \frac{1}{2}
    \dot{\psi}^{\,2}
    \left(
        \sin^2\theta
        (I_{\mathrm{w}:1}+I_{\mathrm{b}:1} + m_{\mathrm b}\ell^2)
        +\cos^2\theta(I_{\mathrm{w}:3}+I_{\mathrm{b}:3})
    \right).
    \label{eq:constrained_kinetic_energy}
\end{align}

\subsection{Potential Energy}

The gravitational potential energy of the pendulum body is
\begin{equation}
    V
    =
    m_{\mathrm b}g z_{\mathrm b}.
    \label{eq:potential_general}
\end{equation}

Substituting \eqref{eq:body_position} gives
\begin{equation}
    V
    =
    m_{\mathrm b}g
    \left(
        z+\ell\cos\theta
    \right).
    \label{eq:potential_full}
\end{equation}

Since $z$ is constant, the term $m_{\mathrm b}gz$ is constant and does
not affect the equations of motion. It can therefore be omitted,
yielding
\begin{equation}
    V
    =
    m_{\mathrm b}g\ell\cos\theta.
    \label{eq:potential_energy}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Constrained Lagrangian}

Using \eqref{eq:constrained_kinetic_energy} and
\eqref{eq:potential_energy}, the constrained Lagrangian is
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}
    \left(
        m_{\mathrm b}
        +
        m_{\mathrm w}
        +
        \frac{I_{\mathrm w}}{r_{\mathrm w}^{\,2}}
    \right)
    \dot{x}^{\,2}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \dot{x}\dot{\theta}\cos\theta
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    \frac{1}{2}
    \left(
        I_{\mathrm b}
        +
        m_{\mathrm b}\ell^2
    \right)
    \dot{\theta}^{\,2}
    -
    m_{\mathrm b}g\ell\cos\theta.
    \label{eq:constrained_lagrangian}
\end{align}

\subsection{Equations of Motion}

The motor applies equal and opposite torques to the wheel and pendulum
body. Let $M_{\mathrm{lw}}$ and $M_{\mathrm{rw}}$ denote the motor torque from each motor, 
with positive torque defined in the positive $\theta$ direction on the pendulum body.

The generalized motor torque acting on the pendulum is therefore
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\theta}
    =
    M_{\mathrm{lw}}
    + M_{\mathrm{rw}}
    \label{eq:pendulum_motor_torque}
\end{equation}

Because the wheel and pendulum body experience equal and opposite
motor torques, the corresponding wheel torque is
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\phi_{\mathrm w}}
    =
    -M_{\mathrm{lw}} - M_{\mathrm{rw}}.
    \label{eq:wheel_motor_torque}
\end{equation}

After applying the rolling constraint, the motor contributes an
effective generalized force in the $x$ direction of
\begin{equation}
    Q_x
    =
    -\frac{M_{\mathrm{lw}} + M_{\mathrm{rw}}}{r_{\mathrm w}}.
    \label{eq:effective_motor_force}
\end{equation}

The difference in motor torques between the left and right wheels creates a net yaw 
torque on the robot, producing an effective generalized force for $\psi$.
\begin{align}
    Q_{\psi}
    =
    \frac{
    r_{\mathrm w}
    \left(
        M_{\mathrm{rw}}
        -
        M_{\mathrm{lw}}
    \right)
    }
    {w_{\mathrm{axis}}}
    .
\end{align}
The signs in these equations follow the convention that positive
$\theta$ and positive $M_{\mathrm{mot}}$ correspond to clockwise
rotation of the pendulum from the upright position.

\subsubsection{Translational Equation}

Applying the Euler--Lagrange equation to $x$ gives
\begin{align}
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}
    {\partial \dot{x}}
    &=
    \left(
        m_{\mathrm b}
        +
        m_{\mathrm w}
        +
        \frac{I_{\mathrm w}}{r_{\mathrm w}^{\,2}}
    \right)
    \dot{x}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \dot{\theta}\cos\theta.
    \label{eq:partial_L_partial_xdot}
\end{align}

Taking the time derivative gives
\begin{align}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}
    \left(
        \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}
        {\partial \dot{x}}
    \right)
    &=
    \left(
        m_{\mathrm b}
        +
        m_{\mathrm w}
        +
        \frac{I_{\mathrm w}}{r_{\mathrm w}^{\,2}}
    \right)
    \ddot{x}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \left(
        \ddot{\theta}\cos\theta
        -
        \dot{\theta}^{\,2}\sin\theta
    \right).
    \label{eq:d_dt_partial_L_partial_xdot}
\end{align}

Since the Lagrangian does not explicitly depend on $x$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}
    {\partial x}
    =
    0.
    \label{eq:partial_L_partial_x}
\end{equation}

Therefore, the translational equation of motion is
\begin{align}
    &\left(
        m_{\mathrm b}
        +
        m_{\mathrm w}
        +
        \frac{I_{\mathrm w}}{r_{\mathrm w}^{\,2}}
    \right)
    \ddot{x}
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \cos\theta\,\ddot{\theta}
    -
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}^{\,2}
    =
    -\frac{M_{\mathrm{mot}}}{r_{\mathrm w}}.
    \label{eq:translation_eom}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Pendulum Angular Equation}

Applying the Euler--Lagrange equation to $\theta$ gives
\begin{align}
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}
    {\partial \dot{\theta}}
    &=
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \dot{x}\cos\theta
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    \left(
        I_{\mathrm b}
        +
        m_{\mathrm b}\ell^2
    \right)
    \dot{\theta}.
    \label{eq:partial_L_partial_thetadot}
\end{align}

Taking the time derivative gives
\begin{align}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}
    \left(
        \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}
        {\partial \dot{\theta}}
    \right)
    &=
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \left(
        \ddot{x}\cos\theta
        -
        \dot{x}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta
    \right)
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    +
    \left(
        I_{\mathrm b}
        +
        m_{\mathrm b}\ell^2
    \right)
    \ddot{\theta}.
    \label{eq:d_dt_partial_L_partial_thetadot}
\end{align}

The partial derivative with respect to $\theta$ is
\begin{align}
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}
    {\partial \theta}
    &=
    -m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \dot{x}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta
    +
    m_{\mathrm b}g\ell\sin\theta.
    \label{eq:partial_L_partial_theta}
\end{align}

Therefore, the angular equation of motion is
\begin{align}
    &\left(
        I_{\mathrm b}
        +
        m_{\mathrm b}\ell^2
    \right)
    \ddot{\theta}
    +
    m_{\mathrm b}\ell
    \cos\theta\,\ddot{x}
    \nonumber\\
    &\qquad
    -
    m_{\mathrm b}g\ell\sin\theta
    =
    M_{\mathrm{mot}}.
    \label{eq:angular_eom}
\end{align}

The first term represents the rotational inertia of the pendulum
body, the second represents translational--rotational coupling, and
the third represents the gravitational torque.
